# Who can join, and how

Cameroon Open Data is built by Cameroonians for Cameroon. This file explains what that means in practice, what we can honestly promise, and what we cannot.

## Who is eligible

You can join if you have a real connection to Cameroon. That includes people who live in Cameroon, people who were born in Cameroon, people living abroad who are Cameroonian, and people whose family comes from Cameroon. We do not ask for proof of birth, a passport or an identity card, and we will never ask you to upload one to this repository or to a maintainer's chat.

## How membership works

Access is granted by trust and by introduction, in three steps.

1. You ask to join through the application channel published on the organization's profile page. Tell us who you are in a few lines, which part of Cameroon you connect to, and what you hope to contribute.
2. Two existing members who know you, or one recognised institution such as a school, a university department, a cultural association or a community organisation, confirm that you are who you say you are.
3. A maintainer adds you to the repository as a contributor and welcomes you.

If you do not yet know anyone here, say so in your application. A maintainer will help you find a way in, for example by talking with you over a call, or by asking someone from your school or community to confirm your connection.

## What we can honestly promise

No technology can prove someone's nationality over the internet, and we will not pretend otherwise. What we offer is a community that checks itself through personal vouching, a short public record of who vouched for whom, and the ability to remove access from anyone who abuses the project. Membership lists are private to maintainers.

## Keeping the data in Cameroonian hands

The repository is private while the community is being built. Everything you contribute is recorded under the data license described in `DATA_LICENSE.md`. Before any dataset is released to the wider world, the members will decide together what is released, in what form and under what conditions, for example through a gated release where researchers must accept written terms before downloading. No release happens without a public discussion among members first.

## Removing access

A member who uploads private information about others, copies copyrighted material in bulk after being asked to stop, submits deliberately false records, or harasses other members will lose access. Maintainers explain the reason in writing and the member may reply. Removing access never removes the credit for contributions that were accepted in good faith.
